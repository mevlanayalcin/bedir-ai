Live demo

The demo draws on 41 verses and 70 authentic hadiths about the Battle of Badr. Each number in the answer reveals the exact text being quoted and where it comes from.

Bedir AI is an information tool and does not issue fatwas. For a binding religious ruling, consult a qualified scholar.

Why Bedir AI?

General-purpose chatbots can easily make up sources when asked about religious texts. In Bedir AI, answers are written only from the source texts given to the model, and every claim is tied to a quotation.

Citations required

The Claude API's citations feature ties every statement in the answer to an exact passage in the source document. The quoted text is not written by the model; it is the original text from the corpus.

Four languages

Turkish, English, German and Arabic. The corpus holds each verse in the original Arabic with three translations, and each hadith in Arabic, Turkish and English.

Open corpus

Every text the demo uses, along with its source, is publicly available in a single JSON file. Each entry can be checked against its quran.com or sunnah.com link.

How it works

1

Corpus

Verses come from the Tanzil Arabic text, the Diyanet Vakfı Turkish translation, Saheeh International and the Bubenheim & Elyas German translation; hadiths come from open datasets of Bukhari and Muslim.

2

Passed to Claude as documents

Each verse and hadith is given to Claude as a separate document with citations enabled. The corpus is cached with prompt caching to keep costs down.

3

A cited answer

Claude answers using these documents alone. If the corpus doesn't cover the question, it says so plainly.

4

The quote, on screen

The interface shows the quoted text and the source for every citation, so you can check the verse or hadith in a single click.
